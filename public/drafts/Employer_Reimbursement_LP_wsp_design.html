Hi [Manager],

I am planning to enroll in the Wharton Online FP&A Certificate (8 weeks, online, starts June 8). It should be covered by our company's education benefit, so there is no cost to the team budget.

Why now: the FP&A function is being rebuilt around rolling forecasts, driver-based planning, and real-time scenario modeling. The program covers exactly those workflows and uses cases from leading top-corporations.

Time commitment: roughly 4 to 6 hours a week, mostly evenings; live sessions are recorded.

Next steps if you are open to it: I would submit through our benefits portal and you would receive a one-click approval request. Happy to walk through anything beforehand.

Thanks,
[Your name]
  • Lead with the business case, not the personal goal. Approvers respond to team upside.

  • Pre-empt the time question; vagueness reads as risk.

Employer benefit questions

The six questions we hear most.

For anything employer-specific, your advisor handles it on the call.

What if my employer doesn't offer tuition reimbursement? +
A few things to check before assuming no. HR pages often bury education benefits under "learning & development" or "professional development reimbursement." Some employers offer ad-hoc approval at the manager level even without a formal program. If neither path opens up, we offer monthly payment plans and alumni discounts; your advisor can walk through both.
How does IRS Section 127 work? +
Section 127 of the Internal Revenue Code lets employers provide up to $5,250 per year in tax-free educational assistance to each employee. Amounts above that may be taxable to you as wages. Many employers offer caps above $5,250 (often $7,500 to $10,000); the portion above $5,250 typically appears on your W-2 as income.
What if my manager says no? +
Manager pushback is almost always about timing, perceived business value, or budget assumptions. The script in Step 03 pre-empts all three. If it still gets a no, your advisor can help reframe with a follow-up email, escalate to L&D if your employer has a separate path, or time the request for the following quarter.
Will this show up as taxable income on my W-2? +
Up to $5,250 in employer-paid educational assistance is tax-free under Section 127. Amounts above that may be reported as additional wages on your W-2. Your employer's payroll team handles the reporting; your advisor cannot give tax advice, but can flag what to ask about.
Can I split pay between my employer and personal funds? +
Often, yes. If your benefit cap covers part of tuition, you can pay the remainder out of pocket or through our monthly payment plan. Your advisor maps the options based on your benefit limit.
What if I change jobs mid-program? +
Most employer reimbursement programs require you to remain employed through program completion (or for 6 to 12 months after). If you leave early, you may owe the company back the reimbursed amount. Read your employer's specific terms before submitting; your advisor can flag what to look for.
